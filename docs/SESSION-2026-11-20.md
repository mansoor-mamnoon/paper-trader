## Mistakes and changes

### Cancelled decisions came back as authority

The manager's prompt contained the original 19 November sales and its note claiming
all positions were sold and the account was moving to 100% cash. It omitted the
subsequent cancellation reviews, which existed only as events. AES's history still
carried the rejected 18-month closing claim. Today's manager repeated it despite
seeing DATE 2026-11-20 and the headline's December 2, 2026 closing expectation
(12 calendar days away). Today's checker blocked it again.

The desk now reads cancellation and exit-review events into manager memory and the
relevant holding dossier. It recognizes the existing event records without rewriting
them. Historical notes and journal decisions are labelled claims and proposals;
the manager receives explicit current cash, ownership and pending orders from the
ledger. Review context is bounded by the evidence cutoff, including manual reviews
of historical orders, and symbol matching distinguishes GE from GEV. A review
outcome supersedes a conflicting proposal but is still model reasoning, not proof
that a particular investment will succeed.

### Correct raw inputs were lost in later summaries

The recorded software tape showed **20 of 151** eligible stocks, median −5.2%,
**10% advancing**, with TTWO and IBM positive. The software scout preserved that.
The strategist nevertheless described software and chips as having zero gainers,
and the manager repeated broad capitulation language. The manager received the
strategist's narrative without the numeric sector tapes.

Raw verified samples now accompany the manager's book and each stock dossier,
including the exit checker. Scout and strategist instructions preserve the sampling
qualification; numeric observations take precedence over model commentary.
The website shows recorded sector samples beside scout entries and stock debates,
including older reports that predate the new dossier field. This makes contradictions
visible; prompt changes cannot guarantee a model will never misstate a number.

### Publication time was mistaken for event freshness

PANW's analyst described a 6.42% jump from a newly published headline, while the
current numbers showed **−5.09%**. The skeptic identified a recap of the earlier
results and acquisition. The exit checker rejected the sale partly because the
same-day headline had a bullish classification and positive FinBERT score, treating
those as proof of a fresh catalyst. Publication and event dates are different;
classifier and sentiment outputs do not independently verify either.

Dossiers and manager/checker instructions now make that distinction explicit and
prioritize the recorded numeric tape over headline price moves. The checker remains
allowed to approve a supported risk-based exit after costs; it need not prove a
catalyst false. This does not establish that selling PANW would have been better.

Several schema-limited fields also ended mid-sentence, including strategy, analyst
cases and the final note. Prompts now request concise complete sentences within the
existing field limits. This is a mitigation, not a new completeness validator.
