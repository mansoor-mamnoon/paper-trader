        How it works

        A fake $1,000, a fixed rulebook, and nothing hidden.

        1. Scan the market

          Once a day after the US close, the program loads a rotating batch of US-listed stocks and their recent daily prices. It always includes anything it owns, plus SPY.

        2. Apply the screen

          A stock qualifies if it costs at least $5, trades at least $1M a day, closes above its 20-day average (which sits above the 50-day average), and gained more than 2% in 20 days.

        3. Queue the order

          The best qualifiers (by 20-day gain) fill up to three slots. An order never fills at the price that triggered it. It waits for a later session.

        4. Fill with costs

          The order fills at the next session's open, with a 0.10% fee and 0.20% slippage against the account. Whole shares only, and no more than 20% of the account in one stock.

        5. Exit by rule

          A close 4% below the entry price, or 8% above it, queues a sell. A 10% drop from the account's peak stops all buying. The experiment ends after 60 days.

        What this cannot tell you

        • Sixty days is too short to separate skill from luck.
        • Prices are delayed and come from a free source with no guarantees.
        • Stops are checked on daily closes, so a gap can cost more than 4%.
        • Real markets add queues, outages and price impact that this model leaves out.
        • The page refreshes when GitHub Actions publishes after each US session. It can run late or fail, and the last good snapshot stays up.

        This is a simulation and not financial advice.
